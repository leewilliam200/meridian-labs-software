How I test

Every page is checked against WCAG 2.2 Level A and AA, the benchmark the Australian Human Rights Commission points to in its guidance on digital access.

  • By hand, with a keyboard only. Can every link, menu and form be reached and used without a mouse? Can you always see where you are?
  • With screen readers. NVDA on Windows and VoiceOver on Mac and iPhone, on your key tasks.
  • At 200% and 400% zoom, and on a phone-sized screen. Does anything get cut off or overlap?
  • Colour contrast, headings, labels, error messages, link and button names, images and their text descriptions.
  • An automated scan (axe-core) at desktop and mobile widths. It catches some issues quickly, but it's only the starting point, which is why everything above is done by hand.

What this isn't

This is testing and remediation guidance, not legal advice or a compliance guarantee. I don't issue certificates or say a website is "compliant" with any law. The report tells you what I found on the pages tested, on the dates tested, and how to fix it.

I don't sell or recommend accessibility overlays or widgets. They don't fix the underlying code, and many people who use assistive technology find they get in the way.
