Why this matters for a US store

People who use screen readers, keyboards or zoom shop online like everyone else. If they can't choose a size or find the cart, they leave.

It is also a legal question. In the US, claims that a website shuts out people with disabilities are usually brought under Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act, and online stores are the most common target. Seyfarth Shaw counted 3,117 website accessibility lawsuits filed in federal court in 2025. UsableNet found that in the first half of 2025, about two in three digital accessibility lawsuits were against companies with less than $25 million in annual revenue, and that ecommerce accounted for 69% of cases.

An audit can't tell you whether anyone will make a claim, and we make no promises about legal outcomes. What it does show is what's stopping real customers on the pages that make you money, and how to fix it. If you've received a demand letter, talk to a lawyer. Our report can help you and them understand what's wrong on the site.

BlockerWCAG 1.3.1, 4.1.2 (Level A)

The size and color pickers have no name

Who it affects
Screen reader users hear "popup button, Medium" with no idea whether it's the size or the color, so some can't choose the product they want.
Check it yourself
On a Mac, turn on VoiceOver (Command + F5) and press Tab until you reach the size picker. Listen for the word "Size".
How to fix it

Questions store owners ask

Why these four pages?

They're where a customer finds, chooses and pays for a product. A problem there stops a sale. Most Shopify stores reuse the same templates, so fixing these pages usually fixes the same problem everywhere. Where that's not the case, the report says so.

Do you need access to my Shopify admin?

No. We test the public store like a customer would. We never place orders or enter payment details.

Is this legal advice?

No. It's testing and remediation guidance, not legal advice or a compliance guarantee. If you have a legal question, including a demand letter, please speak to a lawyer.

What about Shopify checkout?

Shopify runs most of checkout itself, and store owners can only change part of it. We test the entry step: going from your cart into checkout and the first screen, up to the payment step. We report on what you control, such as your cart, checkout branding and any checkout apps, and we point out anything that sits with Shopify so you can raise it with their support team.

What if an app causes the problem?

Reviews widgets, pop-ups, chat and upsell apps cause a lot of the problems we find. The report lists app issues in their own section and names the app. Often the fix is an app setting. If it isn't, you can send the finding to the app's developer, or swap to an app that works better.

Who does the testing?

Meridian Labs. Automated scans find roughly a third of accessibility issues, so every page is also tested by hand.

Can you make the fixes for us?

If you'd rather not do them yourself, we can quote a fixed price for the changes in your theme after the audit. Ongoing monthly monitoring is also available, which re-tests your key pages after theme or app updates.

What this audit isn't.

Honest limits, so you know exactly what you're buying.

A certificate or a legal sign-off.

Our audits are testing and remediation guidance, not legal advice or a compliance guarantee. We don't issue certificates or badges. The report says what we found on the pages tested, on the dates tested, and how to fix it.

"Just add this widget."

We don't sell or recommend accessibility overlays or widgets. They don't change the code underneath, and many people who use assistive technology find they get in the way. In January 2025 the FTC ordered one overlay company, accessiBe, to pay $1 million over claims about what its product could do.

It also isn't the whole site. The audit covers four pages. Fixing them usually fixes the same templates across the store, and we'll tell you where it doesn't.
